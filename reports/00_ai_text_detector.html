00. AI-generated text detector with word-level heatmaps, from voyage-4-nano

Summary. I fine-tuned voyage-4-nano (12 layers, 189M non-embedding parameters) into an AI-text detector with a document head and a per-token head that gives a word-level heatmap, on one H100 in 0.13 H100 node hours (1.0 GPU hours, including evaluation) at 40.4% MFU (308k tokens/s). On 13 held-out document sets the mean AUROC is 97.4 against 93.3 for the best of the open detectors tried (Fakespot RoBERTa-base); on the 2026 Heterogeneous AI Spans test set it reaches word AUROC 99.98 and document IoU 98.7, and on SemEval-2024 8C the boundary error is 37.0 words. A 512-token document takes 0.17 s on 8 CPU cores and 1.02 s on one core (int8). Main weakness: at a fixed 0.5 threshold human text from some domains is over-flagged (false positive rate 32% on HC3 answers, 76% on SemEval 8A student essays, 2% on MAGE test) although the ranking stays high, so the threshold should be set on human text from the target domain. Details: data in Sec. 2, training and MFU in Sec. 3, document results in Sec. 4, word-level results and heatmaps in Sec. 5, CPU speed in Sec. 6, caveats in Sec. 7. Generated 2026-11-16 19:03 UTC.

1. Design

  • Model. The shipped voyage-4-nano weights (/data/new/prod_models/voyage-4-nano, Qwen3 layers, hidden size 1,024, 16 query and 8 key-value heads, 12 layers). The 2,048-dimension embedding projection is dropped. A linear token head maps every final hidden state to a logit for “this token is AI-written”; a linear document head acts on the mean of the hidden states. Attention is bidirectional, so a word is judged with the text before and after it (Table 4 shows causal attention is worse for heatmaps).
  • Labels. Every training document has AI character spans: none for human text, the whole text for AI text, and exact spans for mixed documents. A token's target is the fraction of its characters inside AI spans. The document target is the mean token target of the cropped window, so for a mixed text the document score estimates the share of AI text (see Figure 1). Loss = BCE on tokens + BCE on the document logit.
  • Word heatmap. A word's probability is the mean probability of the tokens ending inside it. The document score is the length-weighted mean of the document logits of 1,024-token windows (the last window is anchored to the end).
  • Splices. Open data with word-level truth is small. RAID contains a human text and several AI texts for the same prompt, so alternating runs of sentences from the two gives topic-matched mixed documents with exact labels (144,880 in training). The Heterogeneous AI Spans data (Oct 2026) adds real model-written spans in human-origin text.

2. Data

All data is open. Datasets published in the last two months were used where they exist: Heterogeneous AI Spans (2026-10-05) for word-level training and testing, and the TextSight 2026 control set (2026-09-30), which contains output of current frontier models and is hard for older detectors.

3. Training and MFU

One H100, bf16 autocast over fp32 master weights, fused AdamW (betas 0.9 / 0.98, weight decay 0.01, head lr 1e-3), cosine schedule with 3% warmup, lr 1.5e-4, 1.00B tokens (3,814 optimizer steps of 262k tokens; the run was preempted once and resumed from its step-3250 checkpoint with optimizer state), windows of up to 1,024 tokens with 25% of windows cut to a random length between 16 and 1,024. The embedding table is frozen (training it did not help, Table 4).

Documents are packed back to back into fixed 65,536-token micro-batches and attended with FlashAttention-3 varlen (no padding tokens, static shapes so the non-attention parts of each layer compile with torch.compile, fused QKV and gate-up projections). The PyTorch profiler splits kernel time (approximately, since it also counts launch wrappers) into GEMMs 36%, FlashAttention 12%, fused elementwise kernels (norms, RoPE, SwiGLU, residuals) 22% and other 30%. Micro-batch size and autotuning change MFU by under 1 point, and FlashAttention-3 over flash-attn 2 gains 2.7 points.

5.1 How the heatmaps are produced

Each card in Figure 1 has three views of the same text. Words are whitespace-delimited strings; a word's value is taken over the tokens that end inside it.

  • AI probability is the supervised token head: a linear layer on every final hidden state, trained to output the share of the token's characters that lie in AI-written spans. It localizes AI-written text; it is an output of the classifier, not an explanation of the document score. The word value is the mean over its tokens.
  • Feature importance is integrated gradients of the document logit with respect to the input embeddings: 64 midpoint steps (32 by default in predict.py) from a baseline text to the real text, gradient times embedding difference, summed over embedding dimensions and over the tokens of a word. Rose words raise the AI score, blue words lower it. The baseline is the end-of-text token at every position (a zero baseline converged poorly, completeness gaps of several logits, because RMSNorm amplifies near-zero inputs). That baseline reads as AI (+6.7 logits for a short test text), so values are relative to it and most words of human-looking text are blue. The attributions sum to the logit difference between the text and the baseline; the residual ("completeness gap") is printed under each view and was at most 0.07 logits in the examples.
  • Attention is attention rollout: per layer, the head-averaged attention matrix is mixed with the identity (residual connection) and the 12 layers are multiplied; the value is the column mean times the number of tokens, so 1.0 means uniform. It shows where the representation draws its information from, not whether a word pushes toward AI or human, and it is not a causal explanation.

The document logit is a linear function of the mean of the final hidden states, so for a mixed text it estimates the AI share and integrated gradients explain that estimate. Explanations cost one forward and backward pass per step (seconds on one CPU for a few hundred tokens), use the fp32 model, and cover the first 1,024 tokens. Only the probability view has ground truth (dotted underline); the importance and attention views are checked by completeness only.

7. Caveats

  • Human text in the Heterogeneous AI Spans data comes from public-domain books, old JMLR papers, Wikitext and Fed documents, so the model could partly learn modern prose against old prose there. The matched human controls (the same human text with and without AI spans) limit this but do not remove it. Word-level scores on that set are the easiest in this report.
  • TextSight 2026 has 56 AI passages, so its TPR values move in steps of 1.8 points. It uses genre-matched short passages and is the closest check here to current frontier output.
  • Calibration drifts across domains: a threshold fixed at 0.5 gives false positive rates that differ by source (Sec. 4). Choose the threshold on human text from the target domain.
  • The MAGE and SemEval sets share sources with training sets of the same benchmark (new documents, same distribution). RAID unseen generators, domains and attacks, HC3, TextSight and Heterogeneous AI Spans are the shifted checks.
  • Baselines are single checkpoints run as packaged, with 512-token truncation; they were not tuned on these sets.
Computational cost

Everything ran on one H100 (80GB HBM3) at a time through Slurm partition P4. Training throughput 308,255 tokens/s per H100 (40.4% MFU), about 40,224 documents/minute at the mean tokenized document length. Evaluating all sets takes about 3 minutes on one H100.
